# Theming and tokens

[日本語](theming.ja.md) · [README](../README.md#documentation)

The design values: color schemes, shapes, type, elevation, state layers and motion. Every value and its source is listed in [Design tokens](design-tokens.md); where lamina-ui chose its own values is in [Differences from Material 3](differences-from-m3.md). Density is in [Layout](layout-and-adaptive.md#density).

## Color

`lamina/color/scheme.hpp`: color schemes from one seed color. `spec_2021().build(options)` and `spec_2025().build(options)` return the 53 color roles (primary, primary-dim, surface-container, outline, ...) for a seed, light or dark, a variant (TonalSpot, Expressive, Vibrant, ...) and a contrast level, by the 2021 or the 2025 color spec. The 2021 spec is computed by Google's material-color-utilities (`third_party/`). The 2025 spec (`color/spec_2025.cpp`) is a port of that library's Java `ColorSpec2025` for phones, built on its C++ HCT and contrast functions; `tools/color-dump/` checks it against the Java code. Both specs start from the library's scheme for the variant, made in `color/mcu_scheme.cpp`.

The color spec is behind the `ColorSpec` interface, so code that uses a scheme does not change with the spec. The two specs share HCT; they differ in the palettes each variant builds and in the rules that pick each role's tone. `examples/buttons` has a switch that changes between them.

## Shape, type, elevation and state layers

`lamina/tokens/shape.hpp`, `typography.hpp`, `elevation.hpp`, `interaction.hpp`: the corner-radius scale (`corner_radii(corners, width, height)` resolves a `Corners` set to the four radii), the type scale (with the emphasized roles), elevation levels, and the state-layer opacities with the minimum touch target. These foundation tokens are shared by all components; the tokens of one component are in the `tokens/` file with its widget's name (`tokens/grouped_menu.hpp` for `widgets/grouped_menu.hpp`). `TypeRole` has the 15 roles and the 15 emphasized roles of the Expressive type scale (`display_large_emphasized` to `label_small_emphasized`, or `tokens::emphasized(role)`), with Compose's values: the same size, line height and tracking as the role, and weight 500, or 700 for Title M, Title S and the labels. Anything that takes a `TypeRole`, such as `LabelOptions::type`, takes an emphasized role as well.

## Shape library and morphing

`lamina/shapes/`: a C++ port of androidx `graphics-shapes` (rounded polygons with per-corner rounding and smoothing, and `Morph`, which pairs the corners of two outlines and interpolates their cubic curves) and the 35 Material 3 Expressive shapes (`material_shape`). Attribution: [third_party/NOTICE-androidx-graphics-shapes.md](../third_party/NOTICE-androidx-graphics-shapes.md). `build/Release/shapes_gallery.exe` shows every shape; clicking one morphs the large shape into it with the expressive spatial spring.

## Motion

`lamina/tokens/motion.hpp`: the spring, easing and duration tokens of the standard and expressive motion schemes. Each scheme names its six springs by role, as Compose's `MotionScheme` does (`fast_spatial()`, `default_spatial()`, `slow_spatial()`, `fast_effects()`, `default_effects()`, `slow_effects()`). Every widget takes its springs from the active style's `motion` ([Styles](#styles)), which is the expressive scheme (`tokens::motion_scheme`) in the M3 Expressive style.

`lamina/motion/easing.hpp`, `lamina/motion/tween.hpp`: cubic-bezier easing (`ease(curve, fraction)`, Compose's `CubicBezierEasing`) and `Tweened`, a value that moves to a target over a duration along a curve, for the few motions Compose runs as tweens rather than springs: the elevation of chips and cards and the title fade of the two-row app bars. `tokens::CubicBezier` is `motion::CubicBezier`.

`lamina/motion/spring.hpp`: a damped spring with a closed-form solution. `advance` moves a value toward its target by any time step, and the result does not depend on how the time is split into frames. `is_settled` tells when the motion has come to rest.

`lamina/motion/animated.hpp`, `lamina/motion/animated_radii.hpp`, `lamina/motion/lerp.hpp`, `lamina/color/mix.hpp`: a value driven by a spring, four corner radii each driven by a spring (`AnimatedRadii`, never below zero while a spring overshoots; the grouped menu's groups and items morph their corners with it), linear interpolation between two numbers, and color mixing and compositing. `color::source_over` lays one color over another, and `color::state_layer(container, content, opacity)` is a container with the state layer composited onto it: the button, icon button and FAB fill their container with it, since their state layer has the container's shape. A state layer with a shape of its own (the circle of a switch, a menu item, a rail pill) is filled with `with_alpha(content, opacity)` over what is already drawn, which the canvas composites the same way. `color::faded(scheme, opacity)` scales the alpha of every role; the dialog, the menu and the grouped menu paint with a faded scheme while they fade in and out, and the dialog passes it to the label and buttons inside it. An icon button scales its state layer's opacity by the alpha of its content color, so a pressed or focused icon button painted with a faded scheme fades its state layer too, instead of keeping it at full strength.

## Spring model

A spring has unit mass, stiffness k and damping ratio ζ. The displacement y from the target follows y'' + 2ζ√k y' + k y = 0. `advance` uses the exact solution for each case: ζ < 1 (oscillates and overshoots), ζ = 1 (fastest without overshoot), ζ > 1 (slower, no overshoot). Spatial springs (position, size, shape) in the expressive scheme have ζ < 1 and overshoot; effects springs (color, opacity) have ζ = 1.

## Styles

`lamina/style/style.hpp`: `lamina::Style` holds every design value a widget reads, as runtime values. It has the color spec (`Style::color`, a `const color::ColorSpec*`) and one member for each component's tokens (`button`, `icon_button`, `navigation_bar`, `dialog`, `scrim`, `menu`, `list`, `wavy_progress`, ...), plus the foundation tokens shared by all of them: `shape` (the corner-radius scale), `type` (the type scale), `motion` (the motion scheme), `elevation` (the levels, the shadow layers and the elevation tweens), `state_layer` (the state-layer opacities), `interaction` (the minimum touch target and the disabled alpha), `density` (the dense lengths), `gesture` (touch slop, velocity tracking, fling decay and touch scrolling) and `zoom`. A member is either the component's token struct (`tokens::ScrimTokens scrim`) or, where a component has several tables, a set that holds them with the lookups that used to be free functions (`tokens::ButtonSet` with `size(ButtonSize)`, `style(ButtonStyle)` and `toggle_style(ButtonStyle)`; `tokens::ShapeTokens` with `radius(CornerSize, width, height)` and `radii(Corners, width, height)`; `tokens::TypeTokens` with `style(TypeRole)` and `text(TypeRole)`). `Style::minimum_target(density)` is the touch target for a density.

`m3_expressive_style()` is the Material 3 Expressive style. A default-constructed `Style` is the same: every member's default is the matching `constexpr` table in `tokens/`, so the values are written once, in the token headers, and the tables stay the source of the defaults. The free functions in `tokens/` (`tokens::button_size`, `tokens::corner_radius`, `tokens::type_style`, ...) still return those default values; they do not follow the active style.

### How widgets get their tokens

`current_style()` is the active style of the calling thread: `m3_expressive_style()` unless another one is installed. Every widget reads its tokens through it (`current_style().button.size(options_.size)`, `current_style().scrim.opacity`); no widget asks which style is active. Three kinds of values are still read from `tokens/` directly, because they belong to the platform or to the icon font rather than to a style: the shortcut text formats (`tokens::default_shortcut_format()`, chosen by the platform; the menus and `format_chord` take one as an option), the icon font's axes (`tokens::symbol_axes`, the defaults of `icons::IconStyle` and the optical size range of the font) and the density step (`tokens::density_interval`, the 4 dp by which each density step shortens a length, used by `DenseLength::at`; the lengths themselves are in the style).

The active style is a thread-wide setting rather than an argument because widgets read tokens in every phase, not only while painting. A button takes its height, its label's type and its springs when it is constructed, its minimum width when it is measured, its touch target when it handles a press, and its colors and outline when it paints. The color scheme reaches only `paint(canvas, scheme)`, so passing the style alongside the scheme would have left the other phases on the constant tables, and adding a style to every constructor would have changed the signature of every widget. With the active style, no public signature changed. The color scheme stays on the paint path: it is built per app (seed, light or dark, contrast) and is changed per paint, since the dialog and the menus paint their contents with a faded scheme while they fade.

The style holds the color spec rather than a built scheme for the same reason: the spec is the rule that turns an app's `SchemeOptions` into roles, and it belongs to the style, while the options and the fading belong to the app. An app that follows the style builds its scheme with `style.color->build(options)`. The M3 Expressive style's spec is `spec_2025()`. The examples build their schemes from the active style. The examples tuned with the 2021 spec (the components gallery, buttons, navigation, layout, playground, shapes and text field) set `examples::sample_style()`, a copy of the M3 Expressive style with `spec_2021()`, as their default style at the start of `main`; the calculator, the timer and the to-do list use the M3 Expressive style. The buttons example keeps its switch between the two specs.

### Supplying a style

- `StyleScope scope(style)` installs a style until the end of the scope and then restores the one before it; scopes nest.
- `set_default_style(style)` makes a style the app's default: the active style on every thread where no other style is installed. `default_style()` returns it, and `reset_default_style()` goes back to the M3 Expressive style. An app with one style calls it once, at the start of `main`, before it builds any widget.
- `set_current_style(style)` installs a style for the rest of the calling thread, over the default; `clear_current_style()` removes it.
- `sdl3::Window::set_style(style)` gives a window its own style; `Window::style()` returns it, or the active style when none was set. `sdl3::run_app` installs the window's style around each frame's update and paint. An app with its own frame loop puts a `StyleScope` around its update and paint. A per-window style needs the `run_app(Window&, update, paint)` overload, since the overload that takes `WindowOptions` creates its window itself.
- Widgets built before the frame loop, as in the [minimal program](../README.md#a-minimal-program), are built under the style active where they are built: the default style unless a scope or `set_current_style` says otherwise. With `set_default_style` that is already the app's style. Every widget [re-reads its style](#when-the-style-changes) and follows the window's style on its first step, so one built under another style corrects itself.

The style is held by reference: the caller keeps it alive while it is installed. A style is copied and changed like any struct:

```cpp
lamina::Style quiet = lamina::m3_expressive_style();
quiet.scrim.opacity = 0.5;
quiet.button.styles[static_cast<std::size_t>(lamina::tokens::ButtonStyle::outlined)].outline = lamina::color::Role::outline;
window.set_style(quiet);
```

### When the style changes

A widget reads most values when it uses them. Values used while painting (colors, outlines, the scrim, state layers, corner radii computed from the size, the presentation switches below) follow a style installed later at once. Values a widget keeps (heights, the laid-out labels, springs, the corners of a shape morph, the targets of its animations) are taken when it is built or measured, and are read again when the style changes:

- Every `Style` has a revision (`Style::revision`, a `StyleRevision`). A new style, a copy and an assigned style each take a new revision from a counter shared by the process, so two styles never share one, even when one is destroyed and another is built at the same address. A style changed in place keeps its revision until `style.changed()` gives it a new one; call it after changing an installed style.
- `StyleStamp` remembers the revision of the active style when it was made. `stale()` tells whether the active style has another revision now, and `refresh()` takes the new one and returns whether it changed.
- A widget that keeps style values holds a `StyleStamp` and has a public `restyle()`, which reads those values again from the active style and refreshes the stamp. Its `step()` calls `restyle()` when the stamp is stale, so a widget that is stepped follows a new style on its next frame without help from the app; `Label`, which has no `step()`, does it in `measure()`. A widget that holds others calls their `restyle()` from its own. `run_app` installs the window's style before the update, so this happens under the window's style. A program that does not step a widget calls `restyle()` itself.
- `restyle()` keeps the state of the widget: its label, checked state, focus, a size given by `set_size`, and the current value and velocity of every animation. Springs change their parameters and go on from where they are, and animations whose target comes from the style move to the new target, so a style change animates instead of jumping. A widget built under one style and stepped under another paints exactly as one built under the other once both have settled; `tests/restyle_test.cpp` checks this.
- Values the app gave in the options stay as given. Defaults of option structs that are taken from the style when the options object is built cannot be told apart from values the app chose, so they stay too: `ButtonShapes::spring` and the option defaults listed under the breaking changes. A toggle button whose shapes came from the style (no `ButtonOptions::shapes`) takes them again on `restyle()`.

Every widget re-reads its style: `Button`, `IconButton`, `Badge`, `ButtonGroup`, `SplitButton`, `Label`, `Checkbox`, `Switch`, `RadioButton`, `Fab`, `ExtendedFab`, `FabMenu` and its `ToggleFab`, `ListView`, `ListItem`, `ScrollArea`, `Table`, `SegmentedButtonRow`, `Card`, `Chip`, `ChipGroup`, `TabRow`, `NavigationBar`, `NavigationRail`, `SideNavigation`, `DrawerSheet`, `ModalNavigationDrawer`, `DismissibleNavigationDrawer`, `ModalNavigationRail`, `NavigationSuiteScaffold`, `ThreePaneScaffold`, `SplitPane`, `TopAppBar`, `TitleBar`, `FloatingToolbar`, `FlexibleBottomAppBar`, `SearchBar`, `Snackbar`, `Dialog`, `PickerDialog`, `BottomSheet`, `SideSheet`, `Tooltip`, `Menu`, `GroupedMenu`, `MenuBar`, `Slider`, `RangeSlider`, `TextField`, `NumericField`, `DatePicker`, `DateRangePicker`, `DockedDatePicker`, `TimePicker`, `Pager`, `PullToRefresh`, `ZoomView`, `LoadingIndicator`, `CircularProgressIndicator`, `LinearWavyProgressIndicator` and `CircularWavyProgressIndicator`. The helpers they share have their own `restyle()` (`FocusIndicator`, `Enablement`, `AnimatedCorners`, `InteractionElevation`, `PopupTransition`, `TopAppBarState`, `DragHandlePainter`, `SheetDragHandle`, `ContentHost`, and `BottomAppBarState::restyle(limit)`) or a setter (`TouchScroll::set_tokens`, `Fling::set_decay`, `AnchoredDraggable::set_gesture`, `AnchoredDraggable::retune` and `AnimatedRadii::set_spec`).

A widget that holds others restyles them and lays them out again: a dialog builds its layout again, a modal drawer or rail measures its sheet again in its area, a sheet restyles the content it hosts, a three pane scaffold restyles the widget sets of its panes and a picker dialog restyles its pane. `WidgetSet::restyle()` restyles every member that has a `restyle()` (the `Restylable` concept in `widgets/protocol.hpp`), and `DialogPane` has a virtual `restyle()`, which does nothing by default and which `PaneOf` forwards to its widget. An open menu is placed again from the anchor it was opened at, and an open submenu from its item's new rectangle. `Menu::reposition(anchor, window)` and `Menu::reposition(anchor, window, horizontal_margin, vertical_margin)` place an open menu by another anchor without opening it again: a menu bar uses it to keep its open menu under the item's new rectangle, and a split button to keep its menu under its trailing button. A widget laid out by its owner (a segmented button row, a button group) measures itself again, and the owner's next `measure` and `arrange` place it. A split button and a search bar also arrange themselves again in the area they were last arranged in; for a split button this places an open menu by the trailing button where the owner will put it.

Some widgets keep what belongs to their state rather than to the style: a split pane its divider position, a zoom view its scale and position, a date range picker the month it shows (it scrolls to the same place in the new month heights), a flexible bottom app bar how far it is collapsed. A scrollable tab row whose selected tab was centred centres it again; one scrolled elsewhere keeps its scroll.

A drawer, rail or bottom sheet slide that is already running goes on from its position and velocity with the new style's spring (`AnchoredDraggable::retune`); a bottom sheet remembers whether it was showing, hiding or settling to pick the spring. A few things still do not move with a style change:

- A running tween finishes with the duration and curve it started with, and the next one takes the new style's: the settle of a drawer after a drag, the slide of a side sheet, the wavy progress tweens and the elevation tweens of chips and cards.
- A drag keeps the velocity tracking it started with. The trackers of a `TouchScroll`, a `ZoomView` and a `PaneExpansionState` keep the tracking tokens they were built with; an `AnchoredDraggable` takes the new ones on its next press.
- Values a widget passes to a child only through the child's options stay as they were built: the style, size and colors of a tooltip's action button, the content color of toolbar and bottom app bar buttons, the size and content color of the header buttons of a side sheet, a search bar and the date pickers, and the minimum width of a docked date picker's field. The children re-read their own tokens.

Keep a style alive while anything built under it may still use it, also after installing another. `PagerOptions::tokens`, `PullToRefreshOptions::tokens` and `PullToRefreshOptions::arrow_tokens` are null by default, and then the widget reads the active style's tokens at each use; a pointer the program sets stays as given. The fling decay curve (`fling_decay()`) is computed for the active style's `gesture.fling_decay` and kept for later uses of the same values, so every distinct decay is computed once. A `ScrollArea` and a `TabRow` take the new curve on `restyle()`, and a running fling goes on from its current position and velocity on the new curve. A `ZoomView` takes the new curve for its flings on `restyle()` in the same way. A `TouchScroll` outside a `ScrollArea` keeps the touch tokens it was built with. `ZoomViewOptions::tokens` is null by default, like the pager's, and a zoom view then reads the active style's zoom tokens at each use, so its settle spring and its limits follow the style; a running settle goes on from its progress and velocity with the new spring. The loading indicator's shapes and the circular wavy progress indicator's shapes are built for the active style's tokens and kept for later uses of the same values, like the fling decay.

### Presentation switches

Four switches change how a style shows containers, layers, selection and lists. Their defaults are the Material 3 behavior, and a widget follows them by reading their values, never by asking which style is active.

- `Style::containers` (`tokens::ContainerTokens`): `fill` is `ContainerFill::filled` (M3) or `ContainerFill::outlined`. Outlined containers are filled with `outlined_fill` and drawn with an `outline_width` line in `outline`. It applies to the containers painted through `widgets::paint_container`: menus, grouped menus, dialogs, rich tooltips, snackbars, sheets, the modal and standard drawer sheets, the search bar and the floating toolbar. Components whose tokens already have an outlined variant (buttons, cards, chips, text fields) keep choosing it by their own options.
- `Style::layering` (`tokens::LayeringTokens`): `mode` is `Layering::lift` (M3: a surface in front is a lighter container with a shadow) or `Layering::recede`. When receding, the overlays painted through `paint_container` take `receded_container` (the background color) instead of their container color, a menu dims what is behind it with the style's scrim (`paint_receded_backdrop`), and a modal navigation drawer reports `content_shift()`, how far the content beside it should move: `drawer_push` times the width of the open part of the drawer. The drawer still dims the content with its scrim. The drawer does not own the content, so the program that paints the content translates it by `content_shift()`; under `lift` it is always 0.
- `Style::selection` (`tokens::SelectionTokens`): `mark` is `SelectionMark::surface` (M3: a container or a pill behind the selected item) or `SelectionMark::foreground`. With `foreground`, a selected list item has no container and takes the `foreground` color, with its headline in the emphasized type role when `emphasized_label` is on; the navigation bar drops the active indicator pill, colors the selected icon and label with `foreground`, and underlines the label with an `underline_thickness` line when `underline` is on.
- `Style::list.row_dividers`: whether a `ListView` draws a divider (the style's `divider` color and thickness) between its rows. It is off in M3. `ListViewOptions::dividers` overrides it for one list.

The values that only matter when a switch is not at its M3 default (`outlined_fill`, `receded_container`, `drawer_push`, `foreground`, ...) are in `tokens/presentation.hpp` with the switches. `tests/presentation_test.cpp` paints each switch against the M3 default with styles defined only in the tests.

Some tables are derived from others in `tokens/`: the dense lengths start from the components' heights, and many components' springs come from the motion scheme. A `Style` holds the derived values as they are, so a style that changes a height or the motion scheme changes the derived members too.

Not in the style: the raster settings of the window (`tokens::raster`), the frame statistics overlay (`tokens::frame_stats`), the icon font's axes (`tokens::symbol_axes`), the density step (`tokens::density_interval`), the video tokens and the shortcut text formats (`tokens::default_shortcut_format()`, chosen by the platform). The canvas and the text layout do not read the style.

### Breaking changes in the next release

- `lamina::Style` is a new name in `namespace lamina`. A file that has `using namespace lamina;` and declares its own `Style` at namespace scope gets an ambiguous name; rename one of them or qualify it. The layout example's `Style` is now `LayoutStyle`.
- The option structs whose defaults come from tokens (`ButtonGroupOptions::expanded_ratio`, `ChipGroupOptions::spacing`, ...) take those defaults from the style active when the options object is constructed, and default arguments such as the `max` of `badge_count` from the style active at the call.
- `PagerOptions::tokens`, `PullToRefreshOptions::tokens`, `PullToRefreshOptions::arrow_tokens` and `ZoomViewOptions::tokens` are null by default instead of pointing into the default style; null means the active style's tokens. Code that reads these members of default options gets a null pointer; read `Pager::tokens()` or `ZoomView::tokens()` instead.
- Code that reads `tokens::` tables directly keeps the M3 Expressive values when another style is active. To follow the style, read `current_style()` instead.
- `paint(render::Canvas&, const color::ColorScheme&)`, `Paintable` and every widget constructor are unchanged.
- `Style` has a new member, `revision`, which a copy does not copy: the copy takes a new revision.
- The widgets listed under [When the style changes](#when-the-style-changes) read their kept values again on the first `step()` (a `Label` on the first `measure()`) after the active style changes, also when a widget built inside a `StyleScope` is stepped outside it. A program that builds such a widget under one style and steps it under another on purpose now sees it follow the second style.
- `ButtonGroup` sizes its buttons with the new `Button::intrinsic_height()`, the height from the style and the density, instead of the height the button had.
- The widgets in the list above have a new public `restyle()`. `Menu` and `GroupedMenu` remember the anchor they were opened at, and `Menu` has `reposition`. A `TextField` read its tokens from the style active when the program started; it now reads them from the active style, like the other widgets.
- `loading_indicator_morphs()` and `loading_indicator_scale()` kept the shapes of the style active at their first call; they now return the shapes of the active style. The circular wavy progress indicator's shapes do the same.
- `Button` has new setters, `set_padding`, `set_min_width`, `set_icon_size` and `set_checked_overlay`, with which a split button gives its halves the new style's padding, minimum width, icon size and overlay. `WidgetSet` has `restyle()`, `widgets/protocol.hpp` has the `Restylable` concept, `DialogPane` has a virtual `restyle()` with an empty default, `AnchoredDraggable` has `retune` and `BottomAppBarState` has `restyle(limit)`. A `DialogPane` subclass that declares a `restyle` of its own with another signature hides the base one.
- A `LinearWavyProgressIndicator` without `set_width` and a `CircularWavyProgressIndicator` without `set_size` take the new style's width or size on `restyle()`; one sized by the program keeps its size.
- `ListViewOptions` has a new last member, `dividers`. The overlays paint their containers with the new `paint_container`; `paint_surface` is unchanged.
