# Menus

## Dropdown menu

`lamina/widgets/menu.hpp`: the dropdown menu. `open(anchor, window)` places it below the anchor, or above it or against a window edge when it does not fit, and it expands from the anchor: it scales from 0.8 on the fast spatial spring and fades in on the fast effects spring. Items have optional leading icons and hover and press state layers. Choosing an item, a press outside the menu or Escape closes it; `handle` reports the chosen item and whether the event was used, so it does not reach the widgets behind. Keys follow material-web's menu: opened with `InputSource::keyboard` (the third argument of `open`), the menu focuses its first item and shows the ring at once; opened by the pointer, it focuses nothing until Down (the first item) or Up (the last). Up and Down then move between the items, wrapping at the ends, Home and End go to the first and last, and Enter or Space chooses the focused item and closes the menu; `handle(KeyEvent)` returns a `MenuResponse` too, so a key choice reaches the same handler as a click. Escape or Tab closes it without choosing, and with `OverlayStack::set_focus` the focus goes back to the control that opened it. The focused item (`focused_item()`) shows the focus state layer and the ring inside its edges; a press in the menu ends the keyboard focus. Items also have access keys, as Windows menus do: `MenuItem::access_key` names a letter or digit key (any other key throws `std::invalid_argument`), and an item without one takes the first character of its label when that is an ASCII letter or digit, as a Win32 menu item without `&` does. While the menu is open, the key alone (no Shift, Ctrl or Alt) chooses the one item that has it and closes the menu, as Enter on it would; when several items share the key, each press moves the focus to the next of them without choosing, and a key no item has does nothing. A held key that repeats does not choose. The keys work however the menu was opened, and they are shown once the keyboard is used, since Windows hides them until then (`access_keys_shown()`): a menu opened with the keyboard shows them at once, and a menu opened by the pointer fades them in on the fast effects spring when a key first moves the focus or matches an access key. Showing them: the key's first letter in the label, in either case, is underlined, and a label without it, such as a Japanese one, is followed by the key in brackets with the letter underlined, as `共有(S)` in Japanese Windows. The menu is always measured with the brackets, so it has the same width and place whichever way it was opened; opened by the pointer, it draws no underlines or brackets until the keyboard is used but keeps the room for the brackets, so showing them later moves nothing. The underline's offset and thickness are the font's own (`text::underline`), at least 1 dp thick, and the brackets come from `tokens/access_key.hpp`; `widgets/access_key.hpp` holds the matching and the drawing that both menus share.

## Shortcuts, dividers, submenus and the context menu

`lamina/widgets/menu.hpp` also has trailing shortcut labels (`MenuItem::shortcut`), dividers (`divider_before`), commands and cascading submenus (`submenu`), and `open_at(point)` for context menus, which keeps the menu 4 dp inside the window. `lamina/widgets/context_menu.hpp`: `ContextMenuArea` opens a menu at a right click inside an area (`input::ContextClick`) or, while the area has the focus, at a keyboard point on Shift+F10 or the Menu key.

## Grouped menu

`lamina/widgets/grouped_menu.hpp`: the Expressive grouped menu (`DropdownMenuPopup` with `DropdownMenuGroup`s). Each group is its own surface (surface-container-low, Level 2 shadow) 2 dp from the next, with an optional label and divider at its top. The first and last groups round their outer corners to 16 dp and their inner ones to 8 dp; a group that the pointer has left takes 8 dp corners. Items are inset by 4 dp, at least 44 dp tall, and the first and last items of a group round their outer corners to 12 dp. A selected item turns tertiary-container and morphs to 12 dp corners. Shapes move on the fast spatial spring and colors on the fast effects spring. `MenuGroup::selection` decides what a click does: `none` reports the item and closes the menu, `single` selects one item of the group, `multiple` toggles the item; the menu stays open for both. An item can carry a trailing icon and a leading icon for the selected state. It is placed like the classic menu but keeps 8 dp from the window's side edges. It takes the keys of the classic menu, going through the items of all groups in order, and Enter or Space does what a click on the focused item does, so a `single` or `multiple` group keeps the menu open; `handle(KeyEvent)` returns a `GroupedMenuResponse`, and the ring follows the item's corners. Its items have the classic menu's access keys too (`GroupedMenuItem::access_key`, matched across all groups in order), shown in the same way; a key that one item has does what a click on that item does, so in a `single` or `multiple` group it selects or toggles the item, which takes the focus, and the menu stays open.

## Menu bar

`lamina/widgets/menu_bar.hpp`: `MenuBar`, a desktop menu bar of classic menus. Alt or F10 focuses it, arrows move along it and between open menus, Alt and a letter open a menu, hovering switches between open menus, and pressing, dragging and releasing chooses; with a `ShortcutRegistry` its items show their commands' chords and enabled state and run them.
