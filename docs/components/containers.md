# Containers

[日本語](containers.ja.md) · [README](../../README.md#documentation)

## Dialog

`lamina/widgets/dialog.hpp`: the basic dialog. A scrim at 32 % covers the window and a surface (280 to 560 dp wide, 28 dp corners) holds the headline, the supporting text and text buttons at the end, placed with `lamina::layout`. `DialogOptions::icon` puts a 24 dp icon (secondary) above the headline, and the headline is then centered. The buttons flow from the end: when they do not fit on one line, confirm stays on the first line and dismiss moves to the next, both at the end. It scales from 0.8 and fades in. The confirm or dismiss button, a press on the scrim or Escape closes it. `open(size)` covers the whole window; `open_in(area)` and `set_area(area)` put the scrim and the centered surface in a rectangle of the window instead, such as the part below a client-drawn title bar, and a press outside that rectangle does not close the dialog. An open dialog holds the keyboard focus on one of its actions: `DialogOptions::default_action`, or else the first action in reading order (dismiss, or confirm when the actions wrap). Opened with `InputSource::keyboard` (the second argument of `open` and `open_in`), it shows the focus ring at once; opened by the pointer (the default), it keeps the focus without showing it until Tab. Tab and Shift+Tab cycle between the actions only, Enter or Space activates the focused one, and Escape dismisses, as Compose's `AlertDialog` does. `focused_action()` and `focus_shown()` report the state. `DialogOptions::content` (`DialogContent`: a layout node, the `WidgetSet` that holds its widgets and an optional `FocusManager` for their Tab order) puts any widgets between the headline and the actions, below the supporting text when there is some, as the text slot of Compose's `AlertDialog` does. When the dialog would be taller than its area less 48 dp above and below, that slot shrinks and scrolls in a `ScrollArea`; the wheel reaches it through `handle(WheelEvent)`. Pointer events reach the content in its own coordinates, and text events while one of its widgets has the focus; `caret_area()` and `next_wake_seconds()` serve its text fields. Tab goes through the content's focus order and then the actions, wrapping, and scrolls the focused widget into view. `set_action_enabled` disables an action, which the focus then skips. Tab inside the content always moves the focus, so a text field there that accepts tabs cannot type one.

## Bottom sheets

`lamina/widgets/bottom_sheet.hpp`: Compose's `ModalBottomSheet` and the sheet of `BottomSheetScaffold` (`BottomSheetKind`). The sheet (surface-container-low, 28 dp top corners, at most 640 dp wide) has a 32 × 4 dp drag handle and any `ContentSlot`, which scrolls when the sheet is taller than its area. `set_area` places it; its anchors are Compose's: a modal sheet hides at the bottom, opens to half its height (at most half the area) and expands to its full height; a standard sheet peeks `peek_height` (56 dp by default), expands, and hides only without `skip_hidden`. `show`, `hide`, `expand` and `partial_expand` move it on Compose's springs, and a drag anywhere on it follows the pointer and settles with the release velocity, a downward fling near the bottom damped as Compose damps it; when the expressive spring overshoots the top, the container stretches instead of leaving a gap. The modal sheet dims its area with a scrim that fades on the default effects spring; Escape steps an expanded sheet down to half before it dismisses, a click on the scrim dismisses it, and Tab stays inside (the handle, then the content), with the handle focused when it was opened from the keyboard. A click, Space or Enter on the handle expands, collapses or dismisses it, as Compose's does. `BottomSheetResponse::dismissed` reports a dismissal as it starts. The modal sheet is a modal `dialog` named "Bottom Sheet" with a "Drag handle" button whose description names its action and a "Close sheet" scrim button; `drag_handle()` lets a program anchor the handle's tooltip, as Compose shows one.

## Side sheets

`lamina/widgets/side_sheet.hpp`: modal and standard side sheets (`SideSheetKind`), docked to the window edge or detached with 16 dp margins and 16 dp corners, at the end or the start (`SideSheetEdge`). Compose has no side sheet, so the values are material-web's tokens and the Android library's behavior: 256 dp wide, the modal sheet on surface-container-low with a level-1 shadow and its start corners rounded, the standard one on surface. A 72 dp header holds an optional back button, the headline in Title L and an optional close button; the content is a `ContentSlot`; `actions` adds a row of buttons below an outline divider. The modal sheet slides in over 275 ms on the emphasized curve over a scrim, dismisses on Escape, the scrim, the close button or a drag past half way or faster than 500 dp/s, and keeps Tab inside; the standard sheet is a layout node whose width grows as it opens, so the content beside it narrows. `SideSheetResponse` reports `dismissed`, `back` and the clicked `action`.

## Cards

`lamina/widgets/card.hpp`: elevated, filled and outlined cards (`CardStyle`) with 12 dp corners and the colors and elevations of Compose's `CardDefaults` (`tokens/card.hpp`). `CardOptions::content` is any layout node and `widgets` the `WidgetSet` that holds its widgets; the card is as large as the content, draws it clipped to its shape, passes pointer and key events to it and paints the outline over it, as `Modifier.border` does. A clickable card (`clickable`, which needs an `accessible_name`) is at least 48 dp, shows the state layer, raises its elevation on hover with the elevation tweens, takes the focus and clicks on Space or Enter; a press on one of its own enabled controls does not press the card. A non-clickable card is an accessible `group`, a clickable one a `button` (Compose gives it no role). Disabled, the container fades to its disabled color (surface-variant at 38 % over surface-container-highest for filled, surface for elevated; the outlined border is outline at 12 % over surface-container-low, as `outlinedCardBorder` composites it) and the content is painted at 38 %.

## Lists

`lamina/widgets/list_item.hpp`, `list_view.hpp`: Compose's interactive `ListItem` (`tokens/list.hpp`): a headline (Body L), an optional overline (Label S) and supporting text (Body M), and a leading and a trailing slot (`ListSlot`: an icon (24 dp), an avatar (40 dp, an image or initials on primary-container), an image (56 dp, 8 dp corners), a checkbox, a switch, a radio button or a trailing text in Label S). An item is one line (at least 56 dp), two lines (72 dp) with an overline or supporting text, or three (88 dp) with both or with supporting text that wraps; it pads 16 dp at the sides and 10 dp above and below, keeps 12 dp between the slots and the text, and aligns the slots and text to the top once the inside is 60 dp or taller. `ListItemAction` makes it clickable, selectable (a radio button that stays selected) or toggleable (a checkbox that flips); a selected or checked item takes the secondary-container colors on the default effects spring, and its corners morph on the fast spatial spring from 4 dp to 12 dp on hover and 16 dp when focused, pressed or selected, as Compose's `shapeForInteraction` does. A checkbox, switch or radio button in a slot follows the item's state and takes no input of its own. `ListView` shows a list of `ListItemSpec` rows in a `ScrollArea` and lays out, keeps and paints only the rows in view plus two on each side (`overscan_rows`): the other rows keep an estimated height (56, 72 or 88 dp) until they are first measured, so 10,000 rows cost what one screen does (`stats()` counts the rows measured, kept and painted). The selected and checked states live in the specs, so a row that leaves and comes back keeps them. The list is one Tab stop, as material-web's list is: Up and Down move the focus between enabled rows, wrapping, Home and End go to the ends, Page Up and Page Down move by a viewport less 48 dp, the focused row is scrolled into view, and Space or Enter acts on it. `single_selection` keeps one selectable row selected. `top_inset` leaves room above the first row, for content under a top app bar. `ListItemSpec::transparent` leaves out the container, so a row shows the surface it sits on (a sheet or the search view), as Compose's samples do with a transparent `containerColor`.

## Dividers

`lamina/widgets/divider.hpp`: horizontal and vertical dividers, 1 dp of outline-variant (`tokens/divider.hpp`, Compose's `DividerTokens`). A `Divider` is `Measurable`: a horizontal one takes the width it is given and a vertical one the height. `DividerInset` leaves 16 dp at the start, the end or both (`start` is the top of a vertical divider), the divider spaces of the M3 list tokens and material-web's `inset`; Compose has no inset and leaves it to padding. Its accessible role is `separator`, with no name. It has no disabled state, as Compose's has none.

## Scroll area

`lamina/widgets/scroll_area.hpp`: `ScrollArea` scrolls content taller than its viewport (`set_viewport`, `set_content_height`). The offset moves on a critically damped spring (the default effects spring), so it never passes the ends; a wheel notch scrolls 64 dp (`handle(WheelEvent)`, which returns true when the wheel is over the viewport and there is something to scroll), Page Up and Page Down scroll a viewport less 48 dp (`handle(KeyEvent)`), and the target stays between the top and the end, also when the content shrinks, in which case the offset glides back. `ensure_visible(top, bottom)` scrolls as little as needed to show a range, for a row that takes the focus. The content is drawn in its own coordinates between `begin_content` and `end_content`, which clip to the viewport (`Canvas::push_clip`; a clip inside, such as a text field's, nests) and shift by the offset; `to_content(event)` turns a pointer event into those coordinates, or into a leave when it is outside the viewport or on the bar, and `to_window(rect)` turns a rectangle back, for the IME caret area. After the content moves under a pointer that stays still, `rehover()` gives one move event at the pointer, so the hover follows. The scroll bar is a 4 dp thumb (`on_surface` at 38 %) 2 dp from the end edge, at least 16 dp long, that fades in while scrolling and out 1 s after; under the pointer it widens to 8 dp and darkens to 50 %, dragging the thumb moves the content in proportion and a press on the track pages toward it. The hiding wait is reported by `next_wake_seconds()` for `Window::wake_after` rather than by `animating()`, so the window idles meanwhile. Material 3 has no scroll bar tokens; these values are lamina-ui's own (`tokens/scroll_area.hpp`). `set_content_width` lets the content scroll sideways too: the wheel's horizontal axis, and Shift with the vertical wheel when the content is wider than the viewport, scroll it, and a horizontal bar runs along the bottom (`offset_x`, `scroll_to_x`, `ensure_visible_x`, `thumb_x`).

A finger scrolls it too. `route(event, deliver)` takes a pointer event for the scroll area and its content together: `deliver` gives an event to the content and returns whether a widget there holds the press (`holds_pointer()`, which `WidgetSet` gathers from its members), and `route` returns true when the scroll area used the event. A mouse event goes as `handle` did before. A touch is given to the content first; once it has moved more than 8 dp along an axis that scrolls and no widget holds the press (a slider, a handle, an inner scroller that took the drag), the scroll area takes the drag and the content gets a leave, so its press ends without a click. The content follows the finger, a release flings it on Compose's spline decay, a touch during a fling stops it without reaching the content, and the content stops at the ends. `dragging()` is true during a thumb or touch drag, `flinging()` during a fling and `scroll_in_progress()` during either. `ListView`, `Table`, `ContentHost`, `Dialog`, the drawer sheet and the date pickers' lists route their content this way. Values and sources: [Touch scrolling](../design-tokens.md#touch-scrolling).

## Zoom view

`lamina/widgets/zoom_view.hpp`: `ZoomView` shows content larger than its viewport at a scale the user sets, as a photo viewer does: two fingers pinch it and pan it, one finger pans it while it is zoomed, a double tap zooms in to 2.5× about the first tap and a double tap on zoomed content fits it again. The mouse drags zoomed content, a double click toggles the zoom, and Ctrl with the wheel zooms by 1.25 per notch about the cursor (`handle(WheelEvent)`, which leaves the plain wheel to the page). `ZoomViewOptions` takes the content size in its own units and an accessible name; `set_bounds` places the viewport. At scale 1 the content fits the viewport and is centred; the scale stays between 1 and 5. Past the limits a pinch or a pan resists (a rubber band), and on release the view settles back on the default effects spring, which is critically damped, so it does not swing past the limit. A one-finger pan that is released while moving flings on the scroll area's spline decay and stops at the edge. The content is painted in its own coordinates between `begin_content` and `end_content`, which clip to the viewport and apply the offset and scale. `reset()` fits it again on the spring, `zoom_to(scale, x, y)` zooms about a window point, and `snap_to_fit()` jumps.

Touch comes from `Frame::fingers` (`handle(FingerEvent)`), not from the pointer events SDL makes for the first finger, since those stop when the first finger lifts and never show a second one. A program gives the fingers to the view before the frame's pointer events, so that `holds_pointer()` is already right when a scroll area or a pager around it routes the press: it is true while two fingers are down, during a pan or a double tap and during a mouse drag. One finger is not held until it has moved past the 8 dp slop, also on zoomed content. At that point the view pans, unless the drag runs along one axis (within 30° of it), a scroller around the view waits on that axis (`PointerEvent::waiting_scrollers` of the touch's pointer events) and the content cannot move that way: then the view lets the gesture go, and the scroller or pager takes it. So a photo zoomed to its right edge hands a drag to the left to the pager, and one zoomed to its top hands a drag downward to the page. On content at scale 1 one finger never pans, so a page or pager around the view keeps the drag. A leave while fingers are down makes the view let the rest of the gesture go, unless the leave is the one that follows the lift of the touch pointer (the other fingers of a pinch go on). That covers a scroll area or a pager that took the drag, and one that took a touch because it stopped its own fling or settle, in which case the content gets only the leave. A finger that lands outside the viewport does not start a gesture, and a down for a finger the view still holds (its up was not delivered) drops the old one.

For a pager that holds several views: `at_min_scale()` says the content is fitted, `edges()` says which sides of the content are showing (`left`, `right`, `top`, `bottom`, within 0.5 dp), and `can_pan_horizontally(dx)` and `can_pan_vertically(dy)` say whether a drag by that amount would still move the content. The gallery's Viewer page shows a view over a drawn pattern and a pager of twelve drawn photos ([Pager](#pager)). Values and sources: [Zoom view](../design-tokens.md#zoom-view).

## Pager

`lamina/widgets/pager.hpp`: `Pager` shows one page at a time and moves to the next or previous one by a horizontal swipe, the role of Compose's `HorizontalPager` and `PagerState`. It holds the position and the gesture; the program lays out and paints the pages. `PagerOptions` takes the number of pages (`count`, changed later with `set_count`), the first page, an accessible name and `mouse_drag`. `set_bounds` places the viewport, and every page is as wide as it.

- State: `position()` is the page as a fraction, Compose's `currentPage + currentPageOffsetFraction`; `current_page()` is the nearest page and `current_page_offset_fraction()` the rest, from −0.5 to 0.5; `settled_page()` is the page where it last came to rest, and `target_page()` the page a settle is heading for. `dragging()`, `settling()`, `scroll_in_progress()` and `swiping()` (a drag, or the settle after one, not an `animate_to_page`) say what moves it.
- Placing pages: `page_rect(i)` is where page `i` is now and `page_offset(i)` its horizontal offset. A page placed by its rectangle, as a zoom view is, takes `page_rect(i)`; a page laid out at the resting place is painted between `begin_page(canvas, i)` and `end_page(canvas)`, which clip to the viewport and shift by the offset. `pages_in_view()` gives the one or two pages showing; `pages_to_build()` adds one page on each side, so a program can build pages lazily and drop the others.
- Touch: `route(event, deliver)` works like the scroll area's. The content gets each event first, with the horizontal axis added to `waiting_scrollers` while the pager waits. Once the finger has moved more than 16 dp sideways, within 30° of the horizontal, toward a page that exists, and no widget holds the press, the pager takes the drag and the content gets a leave. A drag that first moves more than 16 dp up or down is left to the page for the whole gesture, so a vertical scroll area on a page still scrolls. The pages follow the finger. On release, a swipe of more than 25 dp faster than 400 dp/s goes to the page in its direction; a slower one changes the page once it has moved 60 % of a page, either way. The pager then settles on a critically damped spring that carries the release velocity and stops at the page. A touch during a settle more than 2 dp from its page stops it and drags at once, and the content gets only a leave; closer than that, the settle ends and the touch goes to the content.
- Nesting: a pager never takes a drag toward the outside of its first or last page, so a pager inside another one hands such a drag to the outer one. A zoom view on a page keeps a drag it can follow and hands one it cannot ([Zoom view](#zoom-view)); two fingers always belong to the zoom view, and a finger added after the pager took the drag does not take it back.
- `animate_to_page(i)` slides to a page on the same spring; a page more than one page away is first jumped to its neighbour, so only two pages show. `scroll_to_page(i)` jumps.
- The mouse drags pages as a finger does, unless `mouse_drag` is false. The pager is one stop in the Tab order (`Focusable`): while it has the focus, Left and Right go to the previous and next page and Home and End to the first and last, without wrapping, and it draws the focus ring inside its viewport.
- With tabs: while `swiping()`, select `current_page()` in the tab row and place its indicator with `TabRow::set_indicator_position(position())` each frame, so the indicator follows the finger; a tapped tab calls `animate_to_page`, and the tab row's own spring moves the indicator, so the selection does not pass through the tabs in between. The program switches its input, focus and scroll position to `settled_page()` when that changes.

The gallery's sixteen pages are a pager under its page tabs, with `mouse_drag` off: a text field selects text by a mouse drag and does not hold the press, so a mouse drag would turn the page instead. Each page keeps its own vertical scroll area. The Viewer page has a pager of twelve photos, drawn shapes with a number, each a `ZoomView` built when its page comes within one page of the view and dropped when it leaves; a photo that is not the settled one is fitted again. Values and sources: [Pager](../design-tokens.md#pager).
