## Snackbar

`lamina/widgets/snackbar.hpp`: the snackbar, a short message at the bottom of the window with an optional text-button action (`SnackbarOptions::action_label`). `set_area(rect)` gives the space it sits in: it is placed 12 dp above the bottom, centered, as wide as the space allows up to 600 dp, 48 dp tall for one line of Body M (68 dp for two), on `inverse_surface` with 4 dp corners and a Level 3 shadow; the message is `inverse_on_surface` and the action `inverse_primary`. `show(message)` scales it in from 0.8 on the fast spatial spring and fades it in on the fast effects spring, as Compose's `SnackbarHost` does, and restarts its time; it hides itself after `SnackbarOptions::duration_seconds` (4 s by default, Compose's short duration), and the time does not run while the pointer is over it. The time left is a wake time (`next_wake_seconds()`), not an animation, so the window idles while it waits. `handle(PointerEvent)` returns a `SnackbarResponse` with `action` when the action was clicked (the snackbar then closes) and `consumed` while the pointer is over it, so it works as a modeless overlay on `OverlayStack`. The action takes the keyboard focus: the snackbar is `Focusable` and `FocusGated` (`can_focus()` while it is shown and has an action), so a program adds it to its `FocusManager`; Space or Enter on the focused action makes `handle(KeyEvent)` return the same `SnackbarResponse` as a click and closes it, and the time does not run while the action has the focus, as it does not while the pointer is over the snackbar, so it never disappears from under the keyboard (WCAG 2.2.1, Timing Adjustable). Closing drops the focus; where it goes next is the program's choice.

## Circular progress indicator

`lamina/widgets/progress_indicator.hpp`: the Expressive circular progress indicator (`CircularProgressIndicator`). The indicator (primary) runs clockwise from the top and the track (secondary container) takes the rest, 4 dp away at each end; at zero progress the track closes into a circle. Between 10 % and 95 % progress the indicator becomes a wave that moves one wavelength per second, as Compose's `CircularWavyProgressIndicator` does, but the wave is a sine of the arc length with its amplitude and wavelength in dp, so a large ring keeps the token's gentle wave instead of the deeper one Compose's star morph would give it (`docs/design-tokens.md`). The amplitude grows and flattens on the slow effects spring; `set_wavy(false)` flattens it, and with a flat wave and nothing else moving the indicator stops animating. `set_progress` follows the value at once, for progress that changes every frame; `ease_progress` glides to a new value on the default spatial spring while still ending exactly on it. `set_colors` cross-fades to other roles. The size (48 dp by default) follows `set_size`, and the thickness, gap, amplitude and wavelength are options with the token values as defaults. The timer example draws its ring with it.

## Loading indicator

`LoadingIndicator` (`lamina/widgets/loading_indicator.hpp`) morphs through seven Material shapes in a 48 dp container while it is indeterminate, a new morph every 650 ms, and grows from a circle to SoftBurst as a determinate progress goes from 0 to 1 (`LoadingIndicatorOptions::progress`, `set_progress`). `contained` puts it on primary-container; `size` scales the whole indicator. A full determinate indicator that turns indeterminate (`set_progress(std::nullopt)`) keeps its turn, and its size and centre blend into the indeterminate ones on the default effects spring, so the shape does not jump. Values: [Loading indicator](../design-tokens.md#loading-indicator).

## Wavy progress indicators

`LinearWavyProgressIndicator` and `CircularWavyProgressIndicator` (`lamina/widgets/wavy_progress.hpp`) draw the Expressive wavy indicators: linear 240 × 10 dp by default, circular 48 dp, both with 4 dp strokes and a 4 dp gap to the track. Without `WavyProgressOptions::progress` they are indeterminate; `set_progress` hands them over to a determinate value without a jump. The wave flattens near 0 and 1 (`wavy_indicator_amplitude`). Values: [Wavy progress indicators](../design-tokens.md#wavy-progress-indicators).

## Pull to refresh

`lamina/widgets/pull_to_refresh.hpp`: `PullToRefresh` is the Material pull-to-refresh with the Expressive loading indicator, the role of Compose's `PullToRefreshBox` with `PullToRefreshDefaults.LoadingIndicator`. It does not hold the list: the program gives it the list's viewport (`set_area`), the pointer events before the list gets them and whether the list is at its top, and paints it after the list.

- Pull: a touch that goes down inside the area while the list is at its top (`handle(event, at_top)`, true within `top_tolerance` of the top) is watched. Once it has moved more than `slop` (8 dp) down from where the list was at its top, and stays within `max_angle_degrees` (45°) of straight down, the pull takes it: the response has `claimed` (the program then cancels the list's press and its scroll, so nothing clicks and nothing flings) and `consumed` for every event of the touch from then on (`holds_pointer()` is true). A list that reaches its top during a scroll drag starts counting from that point, so one drag can scroll up to the top and go on into a pull. A drag up, a drag to the side, a list away from its top, a mouse or a pen does nothing: lamina does not scroll by mouse drag, so on the desktop the pull is by touch only.
- Resistance: half the finger's travel (`drag_multiplier`) is the pull. Up to the `threshold` (80 dp) the indicator follows it one for one; past it the extra is damped by `x - x² / 4` of the overshoot over the threshold, so the indicator slows down and stops at twice the threshold, three thresholds of pull in (`pull_offset`, `pull_distance` is its inverse). `distance_fraction()` is the indicator's offset over the threshold.
- Indicator: a 48 dp circle on primary-container with a level 2 shadow comes in from behind the top edge of the area, which cuts it, its bottom at `distance_fraction() × threshold` below the top. Inside it the loading indicator's determinate shape (on-primary-container) morphs from a circle to SoftBurst as the fraction goes from 0 to 1, turns back by half a turn with it, and grows from `start_scale` (0.5) to its full size; past the threshold it turns on by `overshoot_turn_degrees` (180°) per threshold of extra offset.
- Release: past the threshold the response has `refresh` and the pull is refreshing (`refreshing()`): the shape turns indeterminate where it is (its turn, size and place carry over, [Loading indicator](#loading-indicator)) and the indicator settles to the threshold on the default spatial spring. Below it, it springs back to the top on the same spring, without a refresh. A lost touch (a leave without an up), `cancel_interaction()`, `set_enabled(false)` and a second finger, if the program passes it on as a cancel, spring back without a refresh.
- Refreshing: the program calls `set_refreshing(false)` when its work is done, and the indicator leaves upward on the spring, still morphing. It stays at the threshold, spinning, for at least `min_refresh_seconds` (0.6 s) from the start of the refresh, so a refresh that ends within a frame or two still shows that something happened, as X's timeline does; `refreshing()` turns false at once, `spinning()` stays true until the indicator leaves, and no pull starts before that. A refresh longer than the minimum leaves as soon as it ends; its shape turns determinate again only once it is hidden. `set_refreshing(true)` brings it in without a pull, for a refresh started from a key or a menu. While it is refreshing no pull starts, so a refresh is never asked for twice, and the list scrolls as usual.
- A pull that starts while the indicator is still on its way back takes it from where it is.
- `accessible()` is a progress indicator node, named by `accessible_name`, while the indicator shows. `animating()` is true while it moves or spins.

Values: [Pull to refresh](../design-tokens.md#pull-to-refresh). The components example has a demo on its Progress page.

## Tooltips

`lamina/widgets/tooltip.hpp`: plain and rich tooltips, anchored to any widget (`anchor_to(widget)`, which follows its bounds and, for a `Focusable` widget, its focus) or to a rectangle (`set_anchor`). A plain tooltip is Body S in inverse-on-surface on inverse-surface with 4 dp corners, 8 × 4 dp padding, at least 40 × 24 dp and at most 200 dp wide. A rich one (`TooltipKind::rich`) has an optional title (Title S), its text (Body M) and an optional text-button action (`action_label`) on surface-container with 12 dp corners and a Level 2 shadow, at most 320 dp wide. It is placed 4 dp above the anchor and centered on it, below it when there is no room above, and kept inside the window (`set_window`); `TooltipPosition` also places it below, left or right, as Compose's `TooltipAnchorPosition` does, and `TooltipOptions::caret` adds a 16 × 8 dp caret pointing at the middle of the anchor. It scales from 0.8 on the fast spatial spring and fades on the fast effects spring. A mouse over the anchor shows it at once, as in Compose (`hover_delay_seconds` sets a delay), and leaving hides it; keyboard focus on the anchor shows it for 1.5 s; a touch or pen held on the anchor for 0.5 s shows it until it is lifted; Escape, a press elsewhere and losing the focus hide it, and `persistent` keeps it until one of those. The waits are reported by `next_wake_seconds()`. `handle` returns a `TooltipResponse` whose `action` is set when the action was clicked, and the tooltip is `FocusGated`, so a program can put its action in the Tab order. `name_tooltip(widget, fonts)` makes a plain tooltip that shows a widget's accessible name, as the components example does for its icon buttons. Paint tooltips after everything else.

## Badges

`lamina/widgets/badge.hpp`: small badges (a 6 dp dot) and large ones (16 dp tall, at least 16 dp wide, 4 dp inside the ends, Label S in on-error on error), from Compose's `Badge` and `BadgeTokens`. `Badge(BadgeSpec{text, accessible_description}, fonts)` is a dot when the text is empty. `badge_rect(anchor, size, has_content, bounds)` places it as `BadgedBox` does: a dot 6 dp in from the anchor's end edge and level with its top, a large badge 12 dp before the end edge and 2 dp above the top, growing toward the end; with `bounds` it is kept inside them, as the `badgeBounds()` rulers keep a navigation item's badge inside the item. `badge_count(n, max = 999)` gives `"999+"` above the maximum, the style of Compose's samples (Compose itself does not cut the text). `NavigationItem::badge` puts one on a navigation bar or rail item's 24 dp icon, kept inside the item, and `set_item_badge(index, spec)` changes it; `IconButton::set_badge` puts one on an icon button's icon. The size follows the text at once, as in Compose. The badge's description, or else its text, becomes the item's or the button's accessible `description`; Compose leaves that to the program (and `NavigationBarItem` clears the icon's semantics), so this is lamina-ui's addition.
