# Testing

## Unit tests

The tests are in `tests/`, one doctest file per area (`tabs_test.cpp`, `side_navigation_test.cpp` and so on), built into `build/Release/lamina_tests.exe`. `ctest --preset release` runs it with three tests that check the resource build errors ([Resources](resources.md#tests)) , `mcu_tests`, the GoogleTest tests of the vendored material-color-utilities, and `gallery_layout_412`, `gallery_layout_800` and `gallery_layout_1440`, the layout check of the gallery ([below](#layout-check)). `lamina_tests.exe -tc="*scroll*"` runs only the matching test cases.

`tests/support.hpp` gives the shared fixtures: `fonts()`, `icon_font()` and `scheme()`, `settle(widget)` (steps 240 frames of 1/60 s), `click(widget, x, y)` and `count_color`. Widgets are tested without a window: build one, give it a width and events, step it and read its rectangles or paint it on a `thorvg::Canvas` and read pixels.

The test fonts and their license are listed under [Attribution](../README.md#attribution).

## Offscreen capture

The gallery is `examples/components/gallery.hpp`, behind a small host interface, so `build/Release/components_capture.exe` drives it without a window: it renders frames with ThorVG from scripted pointer, key, text and wheel events at a fixed 60 frames per second, finds its targets by accessible name, draws the pointer and the last key itself, and writes the pixels as raw BGRA (`size <scale>`; `still <scene> <light|dark> <scale> <file>` for one frame, with scenes for every page (`chips`, `chips_focus`, `chips_hover`, `chips_disabled`, `lists`, `lists_focus`, `lists_disabled`, `bars`, `bars_scrolled`); `video <scene> <light|dark> <scale>` to standard output, for ffmpeg, with `filter_chips`, `input_chip`, `card`, `list_keys`, `badge` and `app_bar` for the components of the second stage and `segments`, `range_slider`, `date_picker`, `date_range` and `time_picker` for the third; the stills of the third stage are `inputs`, `inputs_focus`, `inputs_disabled`, `dates`, `dates_years`, `dates_input`, `dates_dialog`, `dates_range_dialog`, `dates_docked`, `dates_japanese`, `dates_focus`, `dates_disabled`, `times`, `times_24h`, `times_dialog`, `times_input_dialog`, `times_focus`, `times_disabled`, and `inputs_tooltip`, `dates_tooltip` and `times_tooltip` for the name tooltips on the icon-only segments and the pickers' icon buttons; the stills of the fourth stage are `sheets`, `sheets_modal`, `sheets_expanded`, `sheets_side_modal`, `sheets_side_detached`, `sheets_side_standard`, `sheets_focus`, `sheets_tooltip`, `sheets_disabled`, `drawers`, `drawers_modal`, `drawers_dismissible`, `drawers_permanent`, `drawers_rail`, `drawers_hiding`, `drawers_focus`, `drawers_disabled`, `search`, `search_full`, `search_docked`, `search_focus`, `search_tooltip` and `search_disabled`, and its videos `bottom_sheet`, `side_sheet`, `drawer` and `search`, whose drags and flings carry timestamps so that their releases have a velocity; the fifth stage's stills are `groups`, `groups_pressed`, `groups_overflow`, `groups_split`, `groups_focus`, `groups_tooltip`, `groups_collapsed`, `groups_disabled`, `progress`, `progress_running`, `progress_handoff`, `toolbars`, `toolbars_scrolled`, `toolbars_menu`, `toolbars_focus`, `toolbars_tooltip` and `toolbars_disabled`, and its videos `button_groups`, `split_button`, `fab_menu`, `loading`, `wavy_progress`, `pull_refresh` (a touch pull below the threshold, one past it with the refresh and the leave, and one pushed back up before the lift, on the Progress page's list) and `toolbars`; the sixth stage's stills are `adaptive`, `adaptive_compact`, `adaptive_medium`, `adaptive_expanded`, `adaptive_large`, `adaptive_detail`, `adaptive_supporting`, `adaptive_handle`, `adaptive_focus`, `adaptive_disabled`, `desktop`, `desktop_focus`, `desktop_editor`, `desktop_editor_scrolled`, `desktop_divider`, `desktop_editing` and `desktop_disabled`, and its videos `adaptive_sweep`, `adaptive_panes`, `pane_divider`, `numeric_scrub`, `timeline_scroll` and `density`, and the capture draws the resize and text cursors the gallery asks for; the seventh stage's stills are `files`, `files_file_menu`, `files_edit_menu`, `files_context_edge`, `files_sorted`, `files_resized`, `files_scrolled`, `files_dense` and `files_disabled`, and its videos `files_menubar`, `files_context`, `files_table` and `files_scroll`, which right click, Ctrl- and Shift-click and press chords such as Alt+F and Ctrl+S; the zoom view's stills are `viewer`, `viewer_zoomed` (a two-finger pinch), `viewer_panned` (the pinch, then a one-finger fling to the edge), `viewer_double_tap` and `viewer_disabled`, and its video `viewer_pinch`; the pager's stills are `pages_swiping` (a finger held partway from Basics to Chips), `pages_swiped`, `viewer_photos`, `viewer_photo_swiping` and `viewer_photo_swiped`, and its videos `pages_swipe` and `viewer_photos`; these scenes send `Frame::fingers` together with the pointer events SDL would make for the first finger; `tree` prints the accessibility tree after a scene). It is a development tool that needs no display; CI builds it and runs only its layout check.

`--window <width>x<height>` (in dp) sets the window size of every mode; the default is 1440 × 860 dp. A page scene first scrolls its page tab into view with the wheel; any other target that has scrolled out of view stops the scene with "… is out of view" instead of clicking empty space. The stills `scrolled` (wheels the Basics page down) and `focus_reveal` (tabs to the Name field, which scrolls it into view) show the page scrolling. The scenes `gallery_pages` (clicks through seven pages), `gallery_pages_keys` (walks the page tabs with the arrow keys) and `gallery_resize` (narrows the window to just below each window size class breakpoint and to 360 dp, then widens it again; the space outside the window is painted grey) are for recording the gallery itself. `times_resize` narrows the Times page from the window's width to 360 dp and widens it again; the stills `times_side_scrolled` (shift and the wheel over the horizontal time picker) and `times_side_focus` (Tab to its dial) show its sideways scrolling.

Example: one frame of the Basics page at a phone width, and a video for ffmpeg:

```
components_capture --window 412x860 still default light 1 basics.bgra
components_capture video gallery_resize dark 1 | ffmpeg -f rawvideo -pix_fmt bgra -s 1440x860 -r 60 -i - out.mp4
```

`components_capture size <scale>` prints the pixel size of a frame for the same options.

`components_capture bench <scene> <light|dark> <scale> <start dp> <distance dp> <seconds> [repeats] [out.bgra]` times the drawing of a page without a window. After the scene it drags the page up by `<start dp>` with a touch (in drags from `bench_from` to `bench_to` of the window height at `bench_x` of its width, each held and settled so that it does not fling), then drags it `<distance dp>` up in `<seconds>` and holds the finger there, `repeats` times, alternately up and back down, and prints each drag as one line of [frame timing](app-structure.md#frame-timing). Only the frames while the finger moves are measured. `update`, `paint`, `raster update` and `raster draw` are measured as in the app; `upload` and `present` are zero, and the interval is the time from the end of one frame to the end of the next, since nothing waits for the display. With `out.bgra` it writes the last frame. At the size of a phone (1080 × 2400 px at 2.625):

```
components_capture --window 412x915 bench default light 2.625 0 300 1 4
components_capture --window 412x915 bench default light 2.625 1200 300 1 4
```

The first drags the top of Basics with the FABs in view, the second the Type and Theme sections. The frames are drawn on the desktop's CPU, so the times are for comparing changes, not a phone's times.

`--full-repaint` turns [damage tracking](app-structure.md#damage-tracking) off, so each frame is rasterized whole, for comparing times. `--verify-repaint`, with `bench` or `video`, prints a line for every frame: its number, a 64-bit hash of the whole buffer (`render::Fingerprint`), the pixels repainted, and the pixels that changed since the previous frame outside the rectangles the canvas reported as repainted, which the window would not upload; `video` then writes no video. It ends with a `verify` line and exits with 1 when any pixel changed outside the rectangles. The scripted frames are the same from run to run, so running a scene with and without `--full-repaint` and comparing the hashes frame by frame shows whether damage tracking drew the same pixels as a full repaint. Painting each frame twice in one run would not show it, since the gallery's `paint` steps its animations.

### Layout check

`components_capture [--window <width>x<height>] check` opens each of the sixteen pages in turn, lets it settle and reports every widget that passes the window's right edge, has been squeezed to nothing in one direction, or overlaps a widget of another block. A block is a section, caption or item the page places (`Gallery::layout_blocks`); widgets inside one block, such as the parts of a frame, are not compared with each other. It exits with 1 when it finds a problem. Components that cannot fit a window can be listed in `CaptureStyle::known_overflows`; they are printed as known and not counted while they start at the content's left edge, and the check fails if one is no longer on its page. The list is empty: at 412 dp the segmented rows and split buttons share the width there is, and the horizontal time picker scrolls sideways inside its card. After the pages, the check also taps `CaptureStyle::redraw_target` (Send on Basics) with a touch whose down, up and leave arrive in one frame, runs frames while the gallery animates, and then compares the idle frame with one more frame without input; it reports a problem when they differ by more than `CaptureStyle::redraw_tolerance` levels, since the frame loop would then show the idle frame until the next event ([App structure](app-structure.md)). It then flings the page up with a touch `CaptureStyle::fling_redraw_flings` times (from `fling_redraw_from` to `fling_redraw_to` of the window height in `fling_redraw_seconds`), lets each fling settle and compares the idle frame with the next one in the same way. Before the gallery laid itself out again after stepping, the fling's last step moved the scroll offset after the layout of that frame, so the idle frame showed the page where that step began (253 levels apart at 412 dp), and on a phone the page stayed drawn short of the end until the scrollbar's hide timer woke the window. It checks Back three times on a fresh gallery: with nothing open the first Back is handed to the host (`GalleryHost::system_back`); after Tab and Enter on `CaptureStyle::back_menu` the first Back closes the menu and only the second is handed on; after Tab to `CaptureStyle::back_field` the first Back takes the focus from the field and only the second is handed on. On every page of the check, with nothing open, the first Back is handed on. After Tab and Enter on `CaptureStyle::back_dialog` and after the `CaptureStyle::back_sheet_scene` scene (the modal bottom sheet), the first Back is not handed on and a later one is, within `CaptureStyle::back_press_limit` presses. Escape without `KeyEvent::back` is never handed on. It also checks that a gallery tells its host whether it is dark, once at start and again when the theme is switched (`GalleryHost::set_dark_theme`, which the window passes to the system bars, [Android](android.md#system-bars)). CTest runs it at 412, 800 and 1440 dp.

## Touch injection on Windows

`tools/touch-inject/` drives a real lamina window with synthetic touch on Windows, so the SDL touch path, the client-drawn title bar and the touch keyboard can be checked on a machine without a touch screen. It is Python with ctypes and Pillow; run it with `python -B` so that no `__pycache__` is written. `inject.py` holds the logic (finding the window, the guard, `InjectTouchInput`, window capture) and `cli.py` only parses arguments.

```
build/Release/components.exe --title-bar
python -B tools/touch-inject/cli.py --pid <pid> info
python -B tools/touch-inject/cli.py --pid <pid> run tools/touch-inject/scenarios/basics-send.toml --out shots --log shots/log.jsonl
python -B tools/touch-inject/cli.py --pid <pid> run --out shots --step 'action="tap", x=170, y=562' --step 'action="capture", name="switch"'
python -B tools/touch-inject/cli.py --pid <pid> capture shot.png
python -B tools/touch-inject/cli.py --pid <pid> restore
```

The window is the one visible top-level window of the process `--pid`. A scenario is a TOML file of `[[step]]` tables, and `--step` adds one inline table each; steps from both run in order. Coordinates are relative to the client area, in dp by default (`unit = "px"` for pixels), converted with `GetDpiForWindow`. A gesture's path is resolved to screen pixels when it starts, so dragging the title bar moves the window under a finger that keeps its screen path.

| Action | Fields |
|---|---|
| `tap` | `x`, `y`, `hold` (default `timing.tap_hold_seconds`) |
| `press` | as `tap`, `hold` defaulting to `timing.long_press_seconds` |
| `drag` | `from`, `to` (`[x, y]`, or a list of them for several fingers), `duration`, `profile` (`linear`, `ease_out`, `ease_in`), `hold_start`, `hold_end`; with `hold_end = 0` the finger lifts while moving, which flings |
| `capture` | `name`, `settle` (seconds to wait first) |
| `wait` | `seconds` |
| `activate` | brings the window to the foreground and out of the minimized state: `ShowWindow(SW_RESTORE)` if it is minimized, then `SetForegroundWindow`, then minimize and restore if Windows refuses it; waits up to `timing.foreground_wait_seconds`, checking `timing.foreground_polls` times |
| `resize` | `width`, `height` of the client area |
| `restore` | `ShowWindow(SW_RESTORE)`, for after a minimize |
| `watch` | logs the top-level windows of `watch.keyboard_processes` with their DWM cloaked state |
| `info` | logs the DPI scale and the client rectangle |

`tap`, `press` and `drag` take `gap` (the wait after the gesture) and `snapshot`, a list of `{at, name}` captures taken while the finger is down, `at` seconds after it touched. Frames are `injection.frame_interval_seconds` apart; the wait between them sleeps `timing.sleep_fraction` of the time left until less than `timing.spin_threshold_seconds` is left, then spins. Every default lives in `tools/touch-inject/defaults.toml` (`--settings` reads another file).

Before every touch frame the guard checks that the process still has that one window, that it is the foreground window and not minimized, that the point is inside its client area and on a monitor, and that `WindowFromPoint` at the point belongs to it. If a check fails, the fingers that are down are cancelled, the run stops and `run` exits with 3. Captures use `PrintWindow` with `PW_CLIENTONLY | PW_RENDERFULLCONTENT`, so they hold the window's client area only and never other windows.

Findings on Windows 11 without a touch screen (SDL 3.4.10):

- `InitializeTouchInjection` and `InjectTouchInput` work without a digitizer (`SM_DIGITIZER` 0). SDL receives the contacts as `WM_TOUCH`, sends finger events and the mouse events it makes from them with `SDL_TOUCH_MOUSEID`, and drops the mouse messages Windows promotes from touch (`GetMouseMessageSource`). A tap toggles a switch once.
- Hover after a touch; fixed. Observed before the fix: after a tap the cursor (`GetCursorPos`) is at the tap point, and the control under it showed mouse hover until the next touch: after a restore from minimized, after a resize, and with no window change made by the tool. Traced with a message hook (`SDL_SetWindowsMessageHook`, in a build that was not kept): each time, Windows sent a `WM_MOUSEMOVE` at the cursor without the touch marker (`GetMessageExtraInfo` 0), and SDL passed it on as the mouse (`SDL_GLOBAL_MOUSE_ID`). Such a move came between the finger's down and up or just after the up (only one after the up left the hover, which is why it appeared only sometimes), right after a resize or a move of the window (`SetWindowPos`; after a move it lands at a different point of the client area, the same point of the screen), after a restore (following SDL's mouse enter, which makes SDL forget its last position), and when one of the tool's own processes started or ended (by timing only). During a drag, one came at a point the finger had left 10 px earlier. In the restore that was traced the move came from Windows; the source shows that SDL's `WIN_UpdateFocus` also sends one when the window gains focus. SDL drops moves that do not change its position, but these pass, because SDL's touch position has a fraction of a pixel (296.002) and the cursor does not (296). Now the window passes pointer events through `input::TouchEchoFilter` ([Mouse echoes after a touch](design-tokens.md#mouse-echoes-after-a-touch)). With it no hover appeared after a tap, after a wait while other processes ran, after a minimize and restore through the title bar, after a resize, or after the window was moved by 20 × 5 px under the cursor; a mouse move sent with `SendInput` (8 × 4 px, after the same guard checks as a touch) brought the hover back at once.
- A window restored from minimized laid out from a tiny size; fixed. Observed before the fix: with `--title-bar`, the client rectangle of the minimized gallery is 160 × 28 px; after a restore the content sprang back from a collapsed layout and the status read the width class again, and a tap sent right after the restore missed its control (3 of 3). With the system frame the minimized client rectangle is 0 × 0 and the content did not move. The source shows that SDL ignores the size of a minimized window in its events (`WM_WINDOWPOSCHANGED`) but `SDL_GetWindowSizeInPixels` returns `GetClientRect` whenever its width is above 0, and falls back to the last size otherwise; `Window::measure` read that size every frame. Now `Window::measure` keeps the last frame size while SDL flags the window `SDL_WINDOW_MINIMIZED` (`layout::frame_pixel_size`, on every platform): the first frames after a restore show the full layout, and a tap right after the restore hits (3 of 3).
- Two fingers reach lamina as SDL finger events with their own ids (`Frame::fingers`); only the first also arrives as touch pointer events. `scenarios/viewer-pinch.toml` opens the Viewer page and pinches out with two fingers 240 dp apart to 440 dp, flings the zoomed pattern with one finger, pinches in to 120 dp, double taps and double taps again. Observed: the pattern scaled about the fingers' centre while they moved, the fling stopped at the right edge, the pinch in shrank it below the fitted size and it sprang back to fitted and centred, the first double tap zoomed in to 250 % and the second fitted it again.
- Pager: `scenarios/swipe-tabs.toml`, `swipe-photos.toml` and `swipe-keeps-controls.toml`, written for a freshly started gallery at 1440 × 860 dp. Observed: on Basics a 400 dp swipe in 0.25 s went to Chips; a 400 dp drag held still before the lift went back, and a 1000 dp one went on to Basics; while the finger was down the tab indicator stood between the two tabs; a tapped Viewer tab slid in from the page before it. On the Viewer page a vertical drag that started on the photo pager scrolled the page; a swipe went to photo 2; a double tap zoomed it to 250 %; a drag to the left panned it into the rubber band at its right edge, and it settled at the edge; the next drag to the left went to the pager, which slid to photo 3 with photo 2 still zoomed, and photo 2 was fitted again once photo 3 settled; two fingers moving apart sideways zoomed photo 3 without turning the page; a swipe to the right on photo 1 went on to the Desktop page. A drag on a Basics slider and on the Times page's horizontal dial moved them and left the page where it was. Findings: a capture during a drag (`snapshot`) holds up the injected moves for about 47 ms, more than the 40 ms after which the velocity tracker forgets the motion, so a fling with a snapshot has no velocity and snaps back by position; the scenarios take snapshots only in drags that are held still before the lift. A touch right after a tab tap lands while the pager still slides and catches it, so the scenarios wait 0.8 s after a tab tap. In the first run, on a build whose release still counted the up as a velocity sample, the gallery stopped with an access violation (0xc0000005) right after the first swipe; the same steps on later builds, five times, did not bring it back, and with no symbols or debugger on this machine its place was not found.
- Frame timing ([App structure](app-structure.md#frame-timing)): start the program with `--frame-stats log` and redirect its stderr to a file; each swipe or fling that the scenario follows with a wait longer than two refresh periods writes one `lamina.frames: gesture ...` line, so a scenario of swipes separated by `wait` steps gives one line per gesture. `--frame-stats on` adds the overlay, which is drawn in an unrecorded frame after each gesture and does not change the numbers. Do not use `snapshot` in a timed drag: the capture holds up the injected moves (above) and adds a stall of its own.
- No touch keyboard. Observed: tapping a text field focuses it, and the window of `TextInputHost.exe` stays cloaked. The source shows that SDL 3.4.10 has `ShowScreenKeyboard` only in its GDK build, not on desktop Windows. From general knowledge, not checked here: Windows also brings up the touch keyboard on its own for a focused editable element it sees through UI Automation, which lamina-ui does not expose, and by default only when no hardware keyboard is attached; this machine has one, and its settings were not changed, so which of these keeps the keyboard away was not separated.
